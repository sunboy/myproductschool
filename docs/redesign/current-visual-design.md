# The HackProduct Web App: Current Visual Design

HackProduct is a practice gym for product and technical judgment. It serves engineers preparing for PM-style interview rounds and engineers sharpening product thinking on the job, built on a FLOW reasoning spine (Frame, List, Optimize, Win) and coached throughout by Hatch, a non-human robot mascot with a graduation cap. The product is a Next.js 16 app with two audiences and, visually, two distinct token systems that a reader must hold in their head at all times. The signed-in application runs on Material 3 with the Terra theme: warm cream surfaces, forest-green primary, amber tertiary, Literata headlines over Nunito Sans body, and Material Symbols icons. The public marketing site runs on a separate landing-v3 design language: the same forest and amber accents and the same Literata-plus-Nunito type pairing, but delivered through scoped `.v3` CSS variables, serif manifesto headlines, and a glassmorphic marketing shell rather than the app's Material-3 token classes. The two systems rhyme in color and type but diverge in chrome, density, and rendering approach, and several pages sit awkwardly across the seam.

Elevation is done by tonal layering across the six `surface-container` steps, not by hard shadows. A recurring runtime accent is the amber `#c9933a` used for streaks, warnings, and partial-verdict states, slightly warmer than the tertiary token. The marketing landing-v3 system uses its own variable names for the same intent: `--forest #4a7c59`, `--amber #d98211`, cream `--bg #faf6f0`, near-black `--ink-1 #1e1b14`, and paper card surfaces around `rgba(255,253,247,0.82)`.

The one recurring accent color system beyond Terra is the four **FLOW move colors** (Frame forest, List teal/blue, Optimize amber/magenta, Win plum) that thread through steppers, progress bars, competency signals, and step cards across the app. A handful of pages also reach for off-Terra hues: study-plan and module cards use a lavender `#8b46d4` for advanced difficulty, module tracks use indigo and blue dots, and the Skill Ladder uses a real LinkedIn blue `#0077b5` on its share button.

### Typography

Two families carry the entire product. **Literata** (`font-headline`) is the serif that sets every page title, section heading, card title, oversized numeral (scores, XP, prices), and display headline. **Nunito Sans** (`font-body` / `font-label`) is the sans that carries body copy, chip labels, captions, buttons, and UI chrome. A signature device is the tracked-out uppercase `font-label` eyebrow in forest green sitting above a Literata heading. In the marketing system the same pairing holds: `--v3-font-display` resolves to Literata and both the label and the "mono" tokens resolve to Nunito Sans, so eyebrows and "Step N" labels that read as code are actually uppercase letter-spaced Nunito, not a true monospace.

### Icons

**Material Symbols Outlined** (Google Fonts icon font) is used everywhere in the app, with the filled variant (`FILL 1`) reserved for active and selected states. Common glyphs recur as a vocabulary: `play_arrow`, `local_fire_department`, `bolt`, `auto_awesome`, `today`, `timelapse`, `school`, `center_focus_strong`. The marketing pages lean on Lucide icons (Check, Zap, Sparkles, LineChart, Lock, ArrowRight) instead.

### Shape and Elevation

Rounding is generous and stepped: `rounded` (0.5rem) → `rounded-lg` (1rem) → `rounded-xl` (1.5rem) → `rounded-full`, with cards frequently pushed to custom `rounded-[22px]` / `[24px]` / `[26px]`. Shadows are soft to invisible, often expressed as long low-opacity drop-shadows like `0_18px_50px` at negative spread. Depth reads through tonal surface layering, not borders or hard shadows. Glass cards (`bg-white/40 backdrop-blur-md border border-white/50`, or dark `bg-white/10 border-white/10`) appear on hero surfaces.

### The Hatch Mascot

Hatch is the platform's coaching surface, not decoration, and it appears in two forms. In the app it is `HatchGlyph`, an inline SVG robot with a graduation cap and a growth arrow, rendered in one of six animated states: `idle` (greetings, nav, forms), `listening` (reading user input), `reviewing` (grading and processing), `speaking` (coaching and tips), `celebrating` (results and high scores), and `none` (static). State is always contextual, and it is never swapped for an emoji or Material icon. On richer moments a large `MaskoAvatar` video plays (the dashboard hero, tour intros). On marketing surfaces the SVG is deliberately avoided in favor of static Hatch PNGs (`hatch-mascot.png`, `hacky_thinking.png`, blog hero). A few lead-magnet pages call an undocumented `challenging` state, worth flagging in any mascot-state audit.

### The Two Shells

The **app shell** frames every signed-in page: a sticky top pill navigation with streak and XP counters and an avatar, plus a mobile BottomTabs bar. The **admin shell** is a separate two-column frame (fixed left sidebar of icon-labeled nav links on `surface-container-low`, content on cream). The **marketing shell** is `V3PageShell` / `V3SiteNav`: a sticky glassmorphic cream bar (`rgba(250,246,240,0.82)` with 14px backdrop blur), a HackProduct wordmark, a centered row of text links, and an amber "Start free" button, closed by a tall `V3Footer`. These three shells never mix, and which one wraps a route is the fastest way to tell which token system it lives in.

## Global Chrome

**App shell.** Signed-in pages render inside the `(app)` layout on a cream `bg-background` field. A sticky TopNav sits at roughly 67px tall carrying the forest wordmark, a centered pill navigation, and streak/XP/avatar chrome; below `lg` this collapses to a mobile BottomTabs bar. Page content is almost always a centered column capped at `max-w-[1440px]` (narrower on focused pages) with `px-4 sm:px-6` padding. Elevation is tonal, headings are Literata, and forest green is the single action color. Reader articles add a `hidden lg:flex` 248px sticky left `ReaderRail` (a progress-timeline index, dark-glass or light-Terra skinned) aligned to the TopNav's 67px offset.

Two app surfaces deliberately break the shell. The **grading interstitial** and the **live interview room** render full-screen outside the cream chrome: the interstitial is a centered card on cream, and the interview room is a bespoke near-black "dark room" (`#0d1410`) that covers the shell entirely.

**Marketing shell.** Public pages render inside `V3PageShell` under a scoped `.v3` root. The sticky `V3SiteNav` is a translucent cream glass bar (68px, `blur(14px) saturate(140%)`) that grows a hairline bottom border after 8px of scroll: HackProduct wordmark left, a centered row of six text links (Disciplines, Features, Live Interviews, Live Analyst, Hatch, Pricing), and a "Sign in" ghost link plus an amber "Start free →" button right. Below 940px the center links and Sign-in collapse to just wordmark and CTA. The tall `V3Footer` closes every page: a brand column with the mission line "make product and technical judgment trainable" beside five link columns (Product, Practice, Resources, Company, Legal). Pages are single-column scrolls of full-bleed alternating cream and dark bands, each centered by a shared `.shell` wrapper. A hidden `V3AuthGate` modal, opened by every "Start free" button via an `open-auth-modal` event, lives at the root.

## Page-by-Page

### Home

**/dashboard** — The app's home, a server component that fans out into Suspense-wrapped cards inside the app shell (see Global Chrome). It follows a deliberate light-page / two-dark-anchors rhythm across two grid bands, both split roughly 1.08fr / 0.58fr. The above-fold hero is the **CoachSpineCard**: a `min-h-[256px]` rounded-[24px] card carrying a diagonal forest gradient (`118deg, #0f2118 → #284f35 → #4a7c59`) with cream text and three stacked textures (a 5.5% fractal-noise overlay, an amber radial glow bottom-left, a fading dotted grid). A glass "Hatch / today's brief" pill, a Literata greeting, a weakest-competency coaching line with an expandable "Why this?" drawer, a floating MaskoAvatar video, and a four-column stat strip (streak, XP, focus move, done today) fill it. Directly under it, the **ResumeOrStartCard** is the single dominant CTA: a light `surface-container-low` card with a forest eyebrow, a Literata challenge title, difficulty/domain chips, a mint Hatch-insight callout, and one full forest pill button. The narrow right aside holds the UpgradeSponsorCard.

The below-fold band runs a **CadenceRibbon** (a two-pane weekly streak grid plus today's-path step pills), a "Warm up in a minute" heading, and the second dark anchor: the **QuickTakeCard**, a solid `bg-primary` forest card with cream text, a white radial texture, and a flow-driven animated HatchGlyph (speaking → listening → reviewing → celebrating) that runs a 90-second prompt through a grade chip and structured feedback rows. Below sit the DisciplineExplorer FLOW map, an optional featured-autopsy/latest-interview row, EnrolledPlansCard, and a right aside of social cards (AnalyticsLab, HotChallenges, LeaderboardPeek, CommunityActivity, InterviewCountdown). On mobile both bands collapse to one column, the hero drops its avatar, and the AnalyticsLabCard hops into the main column.

### Onboarding and Settings

Two modes: a compact `valueFirst` variant showing only a quick role select, and a wide `sm:max-w-5xl` variant split into a 30% mint `primary-fixed` illustration pane (swapping per-screen onboarding PNGs) and a 70% scrolling form pane. ## Recurring Motifs and Visual Signature

- **Hand-drawn dashed "journey path" SVG textures.** The Explore hub's backdrop, hero pattern, plan-card textures, and module textures all use dashed curved strokes, dashed rulers, and soft circles in a single forest tint. This is the strongest through-line in the Explore cluster.
- **Forest-gradient dark heroes with a spiral and masked dot-grid.** The `#1e3528 → #0e1a14` gradient hero recurs on /explore/plans, /explore/modules, the /progress hero, the /live-interviews lobby, and the marketing autopsy hub, layered with a masked white dot-grid, a green radial glow, and (on plans/modules) a `#7ee099` Archimedean spiral. It is the platform's signature "serious, focused" surface.
- **The light-page / dark-anchor rhythm.** Learner pages are overwhelmingly cream with tonal `surface-container` layering, punctuated by one or two saturated forest surfaces (the dashboard's gradient CoachSpineCard and solid QuickTakeCard, the progress hero, the share scorecard's inverse-surface stage).
- **FLOW as a visible color and structural system.** The four move colors (Frame forest, List teal, Optimize amber, Win plum) thread through steppers, progress bars, competency signals, step-card diamond badges, mini-bars in the interview room, and the calibration `FlowCardFan`. FLOW is the literal spine of the workspace, the debrief hero, and the progress hero.
- **Difficulty-coded generative card art.** Study-plan and module cards carry procedural SVG textures keyed to difficulty (cosine waves for beginner, graded dot fields for intermediate, overlapping chevrons for advanced), tinted green/amber/lavender.
- **The Hatch mascot as an emotional state machine.** HatchGlyph cycles idle → listening → reviewing → speaking → celebrating across grading interstitials, quick-takes, calibration, quizzes, and coaching cards, with the `MaskoAvatar` video on hero moments and static PNGs on marketing. Its state is the recurring cue that the platform is watching and reacting.
- **Glass cards and tonal elevation.** Depth is built from `surface-container` steps and soft long drop-shadows rather than borders, with glass pills (`bg-white/10 border-white/15`) on dark heroes and glass coaching cards on gradient surfaces.
- **Textural overlays as signature grain.** Fractal noise on the dashboard hero, a graph-paper grid on the Post-Session Mirror, `GradingSnow` dots on the interstitial, and the calibration `WorkshopBg` line-art scene all add hand-made texture that keeps saturated surfaces from reading flat.

## Honest Assessment

- **Two token systems create a visible seam.** The app runs on Material 3 Terra token classes; the marketing site runs on scoped landing-v3 CSS variables. They share color and type by convention, not by a shared source of truth, so they drift. Worse, the seam runs *through individual pages*: /pricing and /blog mix v3 hero chrome with app Terra cards, and /quiz/archetype uses app tokens on a marketing route. The forest and amber match today because someone kept them matched by hand, not because the systems are unified.
- **The Explore cluster itself is internally split.** The Explore hub and its inline grid are clean Terra Tailwind with dashed-path textures, but the three drill-down pages (/plans, /modules, /autopsies) switch to a different language (gradient-spiral heroes, generative card art, off-Terra lavender/indigo/blue) built with inline `style={}` objects or a separate `.sc-*` stylesheet. A user moving from the hub into a plan detail feels a subtle re-skin. This is the clearest place the design does well (each surface is polished) and reads as inconsistent (they don't feel authored by the same hand).
- **The dark forest-gradient hero is over-used.** It is genuinely handsome, but it appears on plans, modules, progress, the interview lobby, and the marketing autopsy hub, often with the same dot-grid and spiral. Repetition is dulling its impact; by the fifth appearance it reads as a template rather than a signature.
- **Density and page-shape are inconsistent.** /progress is a long single scroll that the codebase itself notes should probably be a compact multi-column dashboard. /dashboard, /settings, and /profile are tight multi-column layouts, while /frameworks, /flashcards, /history, and the admin pages are quiet header-plus-uniform-grid pages. There is no consistent rule for when a page earns a hero versus a plain header, so some routes feel richly authored and others feel like scaffolding.
- **Several routes are stale, mock, or placeholder.** /domains and /domains/[slug] render nothing (pure redirects), /admin/users and /admin/revenue are hardcoded mock data, and /cohort is a composer where the Stitch reference implies a leaderboard. The CLAUDE.md Stitch mappings no longer describe the live design in multiple places. The strongest, most differentiated screens (the workspace, the interview room, the dashboard) carry the product; the weakest read as generic Material-3 tables and grids that any starter template could produce.
- **The best work is the Hatch-driven, FLOW-structured, texture-rich surfaces; the generic risk is everywhere Hatch and FLOW are absent.** The workspace, Post-Session Mirror, grading interstitial, interview room, and calibration flow are distinctive and cohesive because the mascot and the move-color system carry them. The pages that read as interchangeable (frameworks, flashcards, cohort, admin, the plainer marketing directory pages) are exactly the ones with no mascot presence, no FLOW color, and no texture. The visual identity is real but unevenly applied; it lives in the coached surfaces and thins out everywhere else.
