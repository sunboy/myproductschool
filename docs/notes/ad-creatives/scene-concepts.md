# Full-Scene Hatch Ad Concepts

## Short Critique

The current renders treat Hatch like a small sticker pasted onto a mostly cream typography poster. There is no scene logic, no environmental pressure, and no visual event for a fast-scroll viewer to decode before reading copy. The fix is to make Hatch the actor inside a memorable product-interview metaphor, with props, depth, and a deliberate type-safe zone rather than accidental whitespace.

## Concepts

1. **Giant Hatch Grader** - Hook line: "Hatch has graded thousands. Now it is grading yours." Type-safe negative space: top 25%, a deep-forest interview-room ceiling with spotlights. Hatch towers over a tiny engineering interview desk like a friendly but intimidating expert evaluator, one mitten gripping a huge gold-and-forest stamp frozen in mid-air above a blank answer sheet. The desk, pencils, paper stacks, lamp, and tiny applicant silhouette make the stakes immediately readable: your answer is under review. The drama comes from the scale contrast, the low-angle view, and the paper storm around Hatch, while the upper ceiling stays dark and quiet for headline type.

2. **Boxing-Ring Egg Champion** - Hook line: "Four taps. Your answer either fights or folds." Type-safe negative space: top 25%, a dark arena ceiling with soft gold spotlights. Hatch stands in the center of a practice-ring arena as the referee-coach, raising the mitten hand of a glowing cracked egg champion while a defeated pile of crumpled STAR-method notes slumps in the opposite corner. Ring ropes, paper scraps, corner posts, and abstract audience silhouettes create depth and competition without needing text. The egg becomes the 90-second result reveal, and Hatch becomes the authority that turns interview prep into a scored bout.

3. **Robot Exam Hall** - Hook line: "If your answer sounds like everyone else's, Hatch spots it." Type-safe negative space: top 25%, a high exam-hall wall and ceiling in deep forest. A long hall of identical charcoal robots writes identical-looking blank answer sheets in perfect rows, while Hatch stands on a desk in the center aisle holding up one glowing gold answer card with no writing on it. The perspective lines, repeating desks, and bored robot faces communicate sameness at a glance, and Hatch's brighter palette makes the product promise feel like differentiation under pressure. The scene should feel like a test center crossed with a factory line, with Hatch breaking the pattern.

4. **Egg Wrecking-Ball Breakthrough** - Hook line: "Break through the prep wall in 90 seconds." Type-safe negative space: bottom 20%, a clean cream floor strip with soft shadows. Hatch bursts out of a giant cream-and-gold egg swinging like a wrecking ball through a wall of blank algorithm sheets, interview diagrams, and crumpled prep pages. Shell shards and papers fly toward camera, the rope cuts a diagonal arc through the scene, and Hatch emerges wide-armed with the cap, growth arrow, and H chest mark clearly visible. The image turns the result-hatching mechanic into a single explosive before/after moment.

5. **Answer Lab Distillation** - Hook line: "Bad answers go in. Signal comes out." Type-safe negative space: bottom 20%, a clean lab-floor strip. Hatch is a scientist in a cavernous practice lab, standing on a stool beside a giant glass beaker where dull charcoal answer bubbles, crumpled notes, and abstract interview cards are being distilled into a bright gold droplet. Pipes, gauges, egg-shaped flasks, and green workbenches fill the scene, but none contain readable marks. The memorable beat is Hatch pulling a lever as the golden formula drops into a small egg incubator, implying the product turns messy interview thinking into a precise result.

6. **Carnival Strength Test** - Hook line: "Hit one prompt. See how high your product sense climbs." Type-safe negative space: bottom 20%, a clean cream carnival floor strip. Hatch swings an oversized gold-and-forest mallet into a strength-test machine where an egg-shaped marker rockets upward toward a gold bell. The machine uses only abstract color blocks and tick marks, with no words or numbers, while striped tent shapes, blank pennants, booths, confetti, and shadows give the scene a festive practice-gym environment. The physical action makes "product intuition" feel measurable in one glance, and the bottom strip leaves room for overlay copy.
