Emotional Design Pass · Hatch's honest-not-soft voice

The functional moments are the highest-emotion moments.

Loading, waiting, the wrong answer, coming back after a lapse. LeetCode treats these as dead air. Interview prep is emotionally loaded, and Hatch is already the coach in the room. The question is whether each moment leaves you calmer and more capable, or just more anxious.

The test every moment has to pass: does it leave the user calmer and more capable, or just more hooked and more anxious? Build only the first kind. No streak-guilt, no countdown pressure, no "you're falling behind." That is both a dark pattern and dead against an honest-coach brand. It also happens to be the version that retains, because confidence compounds and anxiety churns.

Pre-live-interview — the most charged moment in the product

The "click start" screen is your "feeder paired successfully" equivalent: the single most loaded second in the whole product. Right now it explains the mechanics. Instead, defuse the dread and tell them what good actually looks like in the first two minutes.

3

The wrong answer — highest retention leverage in the product

A red "Incorrect" is where people quietly churn in shame. Reframe the miss as the product doing its job, honestly: you'd rather miss it here than in the room. Name the specific gap, then offer the same shape again while it's fresh.

4

Coming back after a lapse — do the opposite of streak-guilt

This is where most apps reach for the dark pattern: streak-guilt, "you lost your 7 days," "don't break the chain." The honest-coach move is the opposite. Prep isn't a streak you keep, it's reps you bank, and you still have all of yours.

Rule, verbatim for the style guide: Honest, not soft, and never manipulative. Anxious job-seekers are easy to exploit with streak-guilt, countdown pressure, and "you're falling behind."
